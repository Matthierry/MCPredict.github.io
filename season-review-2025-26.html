MC Predict Season Review: What The First Full Season Told Us

MC Predict was built to be open with the numbers.

The model does not only show the good picks after the event. It makes a prediction on every fixture it receives, and that gives us a much clearer view of what worked, what did not work, and where the model needs to improve next.

This review covers 4,479 match result predictions from 22 August 2025 to 18 May 2026.

Every return shown below is based on a £10 level stake on each selection. The bookmaker return uses the average bookmaker odds available when the fixtures were collected. The Betfair return uses the Betfair Exchange closing price, with 2% commission taken from any profit.

The headline result

Backing every model prediction was not profitable.

Across all 4,479 selections, the model returned:

  • Bookmaker average odds: -£1,544.10
  • Betfair closing odds: -£546.82

That is the first important point. This model should not be judged by blindly backing every prediction. That was never the aim.

The aim is to find where the model has an edge, where the price makes sense, and where future selections can be filtered in a smarter way.

Cumulative profit/loss through the season

Two lines are shown for each group: Bookmaker average odds and Betfair close after 2% commission.

Positive value was better, but not perfect

The main website selections are built around value. In simple terms, value means the model thinks the outcome is more likely than the bookmaker price suggests.

Across the full season, positive value selections returned:

  • 2,120 selections
  • 937 winners
  • 44.2% strike rate
  • Bookmaker average odds: -£706.20
  • Betfair closing odds: -£160.65

That is not a profit, but it is a better starting point than backing every prediction.

Model probability looks important

The cleaner result came from model probability.

The higher probability selections performed better, especially at the very top end.

The best simple filter

The best simple filter from this review was:

Positive value selections with a model probability of 70% or higher.

Strategy comparison

Home wins, away wins and draws

The model predicted home wins far more often than anything else.

The draw sample is too small to take much from. Only 15 draw predictions were made across the full season, so it should be treated as noise rather than a real finding.

What we learned

The season gave us a few clear lessons.

Backing every prediction is not the right way to use the model.

Positive value selections were better than the full set, but value alone was not enough.

The strongest positive value band was much better than the middle positive value band.

The 70%+ model probability group was the cleanest simple signal.

The best simple result came when positive value was combined with high model probability.

That is the direction the next version of the model output is likely to move towards.

What comes next

The money bag bands have been a simple way to show where the model sees value.

They are easy to understand, and they still have a place.

But this review suggests they may not be the best way to group selections going forward.

The next step is to test a different way of sorting predictions. One that does not only look at the difference between the model and the bookmaker, but also looks at the strength of the model prediction itself.

That work is already being explored.

The aim is simple: fewer weak selections, better grouping, and a clearer view of where the model is actually performing well.

Past results do not guarantee future results. This review is based on historic model output and historic prices, using £10 level stakes for comparison.
